## 3. What this means for our design

1. **The root weakness is dependence on the operator.** Passes are generated by the lessee,
   weighbridges sit on the operator's site, and GPS and RFID are on the operator's trucks. This
   validates our **independence** principle and the **GB-owned pole**.
2. **Passes aren't tied to a physical passage.** Most documented fraud is paper vs. reality. Our
   pole closes this in **both directions**:
   - a truck observed with no pass → illegal trip;
   - a pass issued with no truck observed → **phantom pass** (laundering material from elsewhere).
3. **ANPR alone isn't enough** (hand-painted plates). This supports **counting separately from
   identifying** and **default deny**.
4. **VAHAN cross-checks for fake plates already exist** (eDetection), so our desk-review step
   can use them.
5. **Weighbridge chips are real and current (2026).** This confirms C3 as a priority and supports an
   independent weight / volume check.
6. **Detection without action fails** (MSS backlog). Our **logged, non-deletable alert review with
   escalation** addresses a documented real-world failure, not a hypothetical one.
7. **Satellite boundary monitoring already exists (MSS).** Our "mass view" should *use or cross-check*
   it, not claim it as new. Our novelty is **closing the loop**: volume extracted vs. trips observed
   vs. passes issued.

Raw detection alone (seismic, fibre) causes false alarms; verification fixes that.

- **Hand-painted plates:** reports say commercial ANPR (built for standard plates) largely fails on them,

## 6. How other countries handle it (international lessons)

Purpose: check whether our core is just "what everyone already does", and look for ideas for our weak spots
(no-pass trucks, quantity, side routes, insiders). For each case: **what failed · what fixed it · what we borrow.**

### 6.1 Japan: industrial-waste e-manifest (JWNET)
- **What it is:** every load of industrial waste carries a manifest. The **generator, the transporter and the disposer each
  confirm their step** in one national system (JWNET), run by a centre designated by the Ministry of the Environment.
  The e-manifest is now mandatory in many cases.
- **Why it works:** no single party can close the record alone. If a handover is never confirmed, the gap shows.
- **Borrow:** **confirmation at the receiving end.** Today our trip ends at the exit gate. Adding the buyer, plant or stockyard
  confirming receipt against the same pass makes the chain multi-party and makes no-pass material hard to sell.
- Sources: [JW Center](https://www.jwnet.or.jp/en/index.html), [E-manifest system PDF](https://jwnet.or.jp/en/assets/files/Electronic_Manifest_System_for_Industrial_Waste_in_Japan_July2023.pdf), [Yokohama: e-manifest mandatory](https://en.city.yokohama.lg.jp/business/bunyabetsu/gomi-recycle/sangyo/haishutsu/manifest/09denshi-manifesto.html)

### 6.2 Japan: Atami landslide (2021) and the soil-fill law (2023)
- **What failed:** the operator brought in **about 1.5× the soil it had declared** and built the fill to ~50 m instead of the
  planned 15 m. Declared figures were trusted and never checked against the ground.
- **What fixed it:** a national soil-fill law (in force May 2023) with permits and heavy penalties. The Land Ministry also
  proposed **digital tracing of construction soil**: IC cards on dump trucks carrying the volume and vehicle data, read at
  the disposal site and sent to a central server. *Whether it was rolled out is not confirmed.*
- **Borrow:** confirms our **"reality vs paperwork"** idea and the need for an **independent volume check** (survey / mass
  balance), not only trip counts.
- Sources: [Jiji: 1.5× declared](https://sp.m.jiji.com/english/show/13428), [Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2021/07/09/national/atami-landslide-soil/), [Soil tracking plan](https://www.inkl.com/news/land-ministry-plans-new-waste-soil-tracking-system-after-atami-tragedy), [Cabinet Office: new law](https://www.bousai.go.jp/kohou/kouhoubousai/r05/108/news_07.html)

### 6.3 Australia: quarries, overloading, "chain of responsibility"
- **What failed:** overloaded haul trucks are a recognised problem. In one prosecution a gravel truck was **120% over** the
  limit, and the company's on-board scales were no defence.
- **What fixed it:** "chain of responsibility" law: the loader, the company, the manager and the driver are **all** liable.
  Load-weighing and 3D load scanning (e.g. Loadscan) are used at sites without full weighbridges.
- **Borrow:** confirms that **overloading is real and common**, so per-trip weight belongs in the core (not rated capacity).
  The liability side is policy, so it is out of our scope.
- Sources: [Quarry: load scales](https://www.quarrymagazine.com/load-scales-offer-measured-approach/), [Boral CoR case](https://www.fullyloaded.com.au/industry-news/1908/boral-firm-slammed-in-court-for-cor-wilful-blindness), [NHVR CoR](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/about-us/safety-campaigns/primary-producers/chain-of-responsibility), [Loadscan](https://loadscan.com/new-law-puts-safe-loading-in-the-spotlight)

### 6.4 China: Yangtze illegal sand mining
- **What failed:** gangs used **lookouts to watch patrol boats**, stopped mining when enforcement was near, dumped sand to
  destroy evidence. **Patrol-based** enforcement was predictable.
- **What fixed it:** satellite + radar + drones + cameras feeding a monitoring platform (e.g. Wuhu found 20+ illegal
  vessels), plus large joint police campaigns (368 ships seized in one 2021 campaign).
- **Borrow:** confirms **always-on fixed sensing** (our poles) over patrols, and that drones must be **unpredictable** (S4).
- Sources: [China Daily 2023 (Wuhu)](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202303/27/WS642105caa31057c47ebb6ba4.html), [China Daily 2021 crackdown](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202103/02/WS603d8a67a31024ad0baac20b.html), [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/politics/article/3125679/china-targets-illegal-sand-miners-stripping-yangtze-river)

### 6.5 Indonesia: SIMBARA (coal, nickel, tin)
- **What it is:** a Finance Ministry platform (2022) linking several ministries' data (mining, customs, tax, ports) and
  tracking ore **from mine to smelter**.
- **Key trick:** it **alerts when a smelter produces more refined metal than the ore shipped to it could make**, a downstream
  mass balance.
- **Results (official claims):** IDR 3.47 trillion from detecting illegal mining; IDR 2.53 trillion more revenue via risk
  profiling of 828 operators.
- **Borrow:** **link to other government data** instead of only our own sensors. In India: the **GST e-way bill** (linked to
  the vehicle number) and **FASTag toll records**. A truck seen leaving with no pass that later crosses a toll plaza, or
  material sold with an e-way bill but no matching pass, gets caught **off site**. *Data access for the GB is to be verified.*
- Sources: [IEA: SIMBARA](https://www.iea.org/policies/25388-simbara-inter-ministryinstitutional-mineral-and-coal-information-system), [Jakarta Post](https://www.thejakartapost.com/business/2024/07/22/new-tracking-system-to-prevent-fraud-in-nickel-tin-mining), [Project Blue](https://projectblue.com/blue/news-analysis/966/indonesia-implements-simbara-tracking-for-nickel-and-tin), [Tempo](https://en.tempo.co/amp/1894331/indonesian-govt-launches-simbara-for-nickel-tin-tracking-system)

### 6.6 Brazil: timber transport documents (DOF)
- **What failed:** **legal paperwork laundered illegal timber.** Permits were approved for areas never logged, and their
  credits covered timber cut elsewhere. In Pará, 96% of timber in the formal chain was linked to permits, but **only 45% of
  satellite-detected logging matched an authorisation**. Documents also recorded transport from sites with **no visible
  logging**.
- **What fixed it (partly):** comparing declared volumes with **satellite estimates**, and linking state and federal systems
  (Sinaflor). The bottleneck stayed **too few field agents**.
- **Borrow:** the strongest validation of our core: **a document not tied to a physical observation will be gamed.** Our
  pass is only issued when a GB sensor sees the truck. Also confirms mass balance (S7) and the need for automation over
  inspectors.
- Sources: [Mongabay: timber credits](https://news.mongabay.com/2024/08/amazon-fraud-101-how-timber-credits-mask-illegal-logging-in-brazil/), [Mongabay 2025](https://news.mongabay.com/2025/04/fake-documents-real-deforestation-drive-global-trade-in-illegal-amazon-timber/), [Nature Sustainability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-023-01189-3), [EIA report](https://eia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Tricks-Traders-and-Trees-Spreads.pdf)

### 6.7 Ghana: tracking the excavators (galamsey)
- **What it is:** a regulation (2020) requiring **trackers on earthmoving and mining equipment**. Machines are registered (DVLA),
  geofenced to their concession, and **can be remotely shut down** if they enter forest reserves or rivers. Imports are held at
  ports until registered (4,000+ held). 1,000+ machines tracked by 2025–26.
- **Borrow:** **watch the digging, not only the hauling.** Register excavators and loaders in the GB registry and track where and
  how long they work: digging outside the permitted pit, or more machine-hours than the trips explain, is a flag. Trackers sit on
  the Vendor's machines, so this is a hardening add-on, not core.
- Sources: [GhanaWeb 2026](https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/NewsArchive/What-to-know-about-Ghana-s-high-tech-tracker-excavators-2031183), [Graphic: control room](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/sustaining-galamsey-fight-lands-ministry-to-track-excavators-control-room-ready.html), [MyJoyOnline: 1,200 excavators](https://www.myjoyonline.com/1200-excavators-undergoing-permitting-process-mahama/)

### 6.8 Fisheries electronic monitoring (Australia, NZ, Pacific)
- **Parallel:** cameras on a vessel the **operator controls**, which is exactly our position inside the Vendor's site.
- **Practice:** a system-health display, encrypted data, the **regulator keeps a master copy** and reviews a working copy, and
  review was brought **in house** for impartiality (AFMA). Independent auditors must have no financial tie to the operator.
- **Borrow:** confirms heartbeat / health monitoring, GB-held original records (S8) and independent review.
- Sources: [AFMA e-monitoring](https://afma.gov.au/monitoring-enforcement/electronic-monitoring-program), [WCPFC EM standards](https://www.wcpfc.int/system/files/Att%2017%20Interim%20EM%20Standards.pdf), [Fisheries NZ](https://www.mpi.govt.nz/dmsdocument/39269/direct)

### 6.9 Summary: what this means for us
| Finding | Effect on our design |
|---|---|
| Paperwork not tied to a physical event gets gamed (Brazil, India passes, Atami) | **Validates the core**: GB-sensor-triggered pass + reality-vs-paperwork |
| Overloading is common (Australia, Atami 1.5×) | **Per-trip weight should be in the core** (gate weigh pad) |
| Declared volume ≠ actual (Atami, Brazil) | **Monthly mass balance** belongs in Compare |
| Multi-party confirmation (Japan JWNET) | **New idea:** receiver confirms the pass → no-pass material is hard to sell |
| Cross-agency data (Indonesia SIMBARA) | **New idea:** cross-check with **GST e-way bill + FASTag** to catch no-pass trucks off site |
| Track the digging machines (Ghana) | **New idea (add-on):** register and track excavators; machine-hours vs trips |
| Patrols are predictable (China) | Validates fixed poles + unpredictable drones |
| Regulator-held master copy, independent review (fisheries) | Validates S8 |

### 7.3 What the evidence tells us
1. **Every Indian pass fraud (R1–R3) exists because the pass is created or cancelled by the operator.** Our pass is created
   by a GB sensor event, used once, and can't be cancelled after exit. That's the single strongest argument for our core.
2. **Vendor-run weighbridges and CCTV fail in practice (R4, R6).** That backs GB-owned measurement.
3. **Detection alone barely works (Colombia: 7–11%).** It must be followed by automatic action (pass refusal, quota stop,
   truck hold) and auto-confirmed alerts that officials can't sit on. That backs our ACT step and the S8 reviewer controls.
4. **Linking to other systems (Odisha, Zambia, Indonesia) is a proven pattern** and supports the e-way bill / FASTag idea.
5. **Gaps in proof:** no rigorous study of gate-counting systems exists; UP's numbers are official claims. Our PoC should
   therefore **demonstrate each real fraud being caught**, rather than rely on outside claims.

## 8. How they tried to close the gaps (the fixes, and whether they held)

For each failure: the **fix actually adopted**, **whether it held**, and **what's left open that we close**.

| Gap | Fix they adopted | Did it hold? | Still open → our answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pass cancelled after the check-post** (Haryana) | Guidelines amended (2021): buyers must get purchases **verified by the mining department within 7 days** + an undertaking; offending units **barred from the portal** (89 crushers); FIRs | Partly: fraud kept reappearing (fake e-rawanas reported again later). Fix is **paperwork + punishment after the fact**. | The operator still creates and cancels passes. **Ours: only the GB creates a pass (on a sensor event) and only a GB pole closes it; nothing can be cancelled.** |
| **Same pass, many trips; fake / expired passes** (UP) | Paper MM-11 → **e-pass with a tamper-proof QR** (2017); **unmanned sensor check-gates** sending evidence to a portal; violation flags for "**multiple rounds on same pass**", expired and bogus passes; **RFID MINETag on 80,000+ vehicles**; no pass for vehicles without a tag; WIM being added | Reported large enforcement numbers (official). **CAG 2024: no input controls on e-pass issuing**; lease holders still issue passes "without actual need". | Check-gates are on **public roads**, so trucks can use other roads; the **lessee still issues the pass**. **Ours: the gate is at the site exit, and pass issue needs a GB sensor seeing the truck enter.** |
| **Coal theft / diversion at mines** (Coal India) | **RFID boom barriers** at entry/exit; RFID linked to **weighbridges, so dispatch is recorded without humans**; CCTV at weighbridges and gates; **GPS + geofencing** on trucks; **area control rooms** acting on alerts; surprise re-weighing; patrols | No outcome data ("hard to quantify"). Works for CIL because **CIL owns its own mines** (operator = owner). | In our problem the **operator is the adversary** and controls power, staff and trucks. The same parts must be **GB-owned and GB-powered**; GPS on Vendor trucks can be switched off, so we count with **radar**, not with tags. |
| **Weighbridge fraud** (industry) | **Unmanned weighbridges**: RFID / FASTag ID + cameras + boom barrier + PLC, no operator (CSIR-CIMFR design; Avery Weigh-Tronix products) | Vendor claims only; no independent audit found | Still the **operator's** weighbridge. **Ours: GB weigh pad at the gate, compared against the Vendor's weighbridge.** |
| **Off-book coal at the border** (Mongolia) | Sales moved to an **open electronic exchange** (2023), auctions; dedicated exchange gateway at the border crossing (2024); **paperless, digitised border checkpoints** | More transparent sales and higher revenue reported (industry / government); U4 warns secrecy remains | Fixes the **sales** side, not the physical count. Backs our idea of **linking to other systems** (exchange / tax / toll). |
| **Detection ignored / too slow** (Colombia) | Studied: disclosure alone → **7–11% reduction**, displacement, collusion | No: **detection without action fails** | **Ours: automatic prevention** (pass refused, quota stop, truck hold) + alerts nobody can dismiss. |
| **Illegal mining zone** (Peru, Operation Mercury) | **Permanent state presence**: 1,200 police, 300 soldiers, 70 prosecutors, mobile bases, 17+ agencies | **Yes while sustained** (−92% mining deforestation); **came back** when presence and budget were cut in 2020; activity **moved** next door | Human presence is costly and fades. **Ours: permanent "presence" by machines** (poles, drones) that don't get tired, transferred or bribed. |
| **Declared vs actual stock** (Coal India) | **Laser scanning made mandatory** for stock measurement (New Yellow Book); drone mapping under consideration | New; too early for results | Backs our **mass balance with GB-run surveys** (S7). |

### What the fixes show
1. **Real-world fixes converged on the same parts as ours**: unmanned gates, QR e-passes, vehicle ID, auto-linked weighing,
   control rooms, geofencing. **That's validation, not a weakness**: the building blocks are proven in India.
2. **What they kept getting wrong:** (a) the **operator still issues the pass**; (b) checkpoints sit on **public roads**, not at
   the site exit; (c) the equipment runs on the **operator's power and staff**; (d) detection depended on **people acting later**.
3. **Our design is the same proven parts, rearranged to remove those four weak points.** That's the pitch.
